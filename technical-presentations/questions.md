# Questions before starting

Read this file before planning a new presentation. Check the conversation, supplied materials, assignment and project instructions first. Ask the user the unanswered questions that materially affect the work, grouped into a concise message. Do not ask again for information already supplied or for approval of an already approved plan. Use the user's language when asking.

## Core questions

1. **Topic and purpose:** What is the subject and the central question? What should the audience understand, remember, or be able to do afterward?
2. **Audience:** Who will attend, what do they already know, and what level of explanation or mathematical rigor is appropriate?
3. **Occasion and requirements:** Is this a technical briefing, design review, training, demonstration, conference talk, class assignment, or another format? Is there a rubric, required structure, institutional template, or mandatory content?
4. **Time:** How long is the presentation, and does that time include questions, demonstrations, or discussion?
5. **Evidence:** What source materials, data, findings, references, or examples must be used? May additional sources and images be researched online?
6. **Language and format:** What language should the slides and speech use? What deliverables and presentation player are needed? Are speaker notes, a separate script, editable figures, a handout, or a live demonstration required?

## Ask when relevant

- What is the main claim or decision, and are there assumptions, alternatives, tradeoffs, limitations, or opposing views that must be addressed?
- What balance of conceptual explanation, architecture, implementation, evidence, results, tradeoffs, and technical detail does the audience need?
- Are there accessibility, visual identity, attribution, licensing, confidentiality, or technical constraints? Are there examples to match or avoid?
- For images, is the preference for original diagrams, computed figures, generated illustrations, sourced images, or a mix? Are there sources or visuals that must be included?
- What is the deadline, and should the outline be approved before slides are created?

Distinguish confirmed requirements from assumptions. If an answer is essential and cannot be reasonably inferred, wait for it before dependent work while continuing independent preparation. If the user asks to proceed, state reasonable assumptions and work; an explicit request to approve the outline still takes precedence. Do not invent findings or treat unverified evidence as established.
