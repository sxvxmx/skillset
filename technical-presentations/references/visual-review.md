# Visual and image review

Choose a visual for its explanatory role, not to fill a quota. Establish what the audience should notice, what every encoding means, and what the image does and does not establish. Maintain the original aspect ratio and crop only when meaning, attribution and relevant context survive. Check the result at final projected size and in each export.

For code-generated diagrams and plots, inspect both the source geometry and the rendered figure:

- Check symmetry where the model calls for it against coordinates or a mirrored comparison; preserve intended asymmetry. Verify node and label positions, alignment, spacing, overlap, clipping, margins, and unintended empty internal gaps. Recompose panels rather than stretching them to conceal whitespace.
- Check every arrow's direction, endpoints, attachment, crossings, arrowhead and label against the stated relationship. Verify positions and signs with a small known case. Confirm that the legend, color, scale, units, axis order and projection match the data and model.
- Inspect the whole figure and enlarged details at final placement size. Check legibility, contrast, accessible encodings, resolution, and whether useful content fills the available space. Keep scientific labels readable and preserve native objects when requested.
- Record source data, model, domain, parameters, computation method and relevant limitations. Check edge cases and numerical sensitivity where material. An attractive pattern is not proof.

For AI-generated illustrations, inspect the actual output rather than assuming the prompt was followed. Verify symmetry, arrows, positions, whitespace, text, objects and their relationships; regenerate or correct defects. Use native editable elements or computed diagrams when exact geometry or scientific relationships are needed. Clearly distinguish illustration from observed or computed evidence.

For images found online, verify the original source and the license or permission appropriate for the intended use, attribution and any required visible credit. Keep a source URL, author, title, license and modification record where available; do not assume search results are reusable. Check the image's authenticity, context, resolution and relevance. Cite data or figures even if reuse is permitted.

Where animation, video or an interactive model is requested, provide a playable artifact, explain what changes and what is recomputed, and test it in the target player. A poster or PDF is static. Note any playback test that could not be performed.
